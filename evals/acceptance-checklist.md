# Acceptance checklist

Record pass, fail, or not verified with brief evidence. Do not mark a check passed from processing status alone.

## Scope and privacy

- [ ] Topic, reference choice, project, and authorized scope are established.
- [ ] Any required batch approval covers the product, disclosed model, and image count.
- [ ] Public-gallery sharing is off before each generation.
- [ ] No unapproved provider, purchase, public publishing, deletion, or account-wide setting change occurred.
- [ ] Asset rights and any requested voice-cloning consent are established; no secrets are saved in production notes.

## Creative production

- [ ] The full character bible and consistent voice/accent phrase are reused.
- [ ] Scene expression, dialogue, and vocal emotion follow the intended emotional wave.
- [ ] Before submission, each image prompt describes one coherent pose with compatible hand roles, natural alignment, and framing that accommodates the interaction.
- [ ] Relevant props have explicit size, orientation, contact points, and plausible support; the prompt contains no contradictory grip or gesture requests.
- [ ] Prompt enhancement was controlled or its exposed text reviewed; any inability to control or review it is recorded.
- [ ] Video movement directions preserve the intended grip and remain compatible with the source pose.
- [ ] The correct scene image is selected and the reference remains in primary slot 1.
- [ ] The reference and every selected scene image, including replacements, were inspected inside VideoExpress with its preview and full-size/zoom/pan controls before use; no image download was required for inspection, and pass/fail/not-verified observations are recorded.
- [ ] Visible limbs, joints, hands, and fingers have no observed duplication, fusion, disconnection, or implausible pose; natural occlusion and individual anatomy are respected.
- [ ] All visible hands were counted across the whole image and traced through wrists, elbows, and shoulders; the torso and frame edges were checked for stray hands, with cropped or occluded hands explicitly recorded.
- [ ] Held objects have plausible scale, orientation, grip, contact, and support; no floating objects or fingers passing through them were observed.
- [ ] Failed images were replaced or corrected within the shared retry/batch limits and rechecked; no failed or unverified image proceeded automatically to video generation.
- [ ] Dialogue is under 100 characters and entered in Actor 1 Script in the Lipsync Audio dialog.
- [ ] Each intended scene completed and was matched to its recorded job or details.

## Edit and quality review

- [ ] Video and audio clips appear in the intended story order without duplicate insertions.
- [ ] Paired trim boundaries and start/end timing match; speech is not cut off.
- [ ] Unnecessary pauses and timeline gaps are removed.
- [ ] Visual continuity, framing, anatomy, and mouth movement were inspected.
- [ ] Each entire generated clip was reviewed separately from its source image, including arm/hand continuity during movement; any unverified playback portions are disclosed.
- [ ] Speech content, delivery, and audiovisual lip-sync were reviewed where supported; limitations are disclosed.
- [ ] The full timeline was reviewed and the correct editable project was saved.

## Export and delivery

- [ ] The intended MP4 was exported at the highest included quality/resolution.
- [ ] Actual duration, resolution, and audio/video tracks were checked with available capabilities.
- [ ] The complete downloaded file exists and opens; required review limitations are stated.
- [ ] The final file or supported preview and usable download location were delivered.
- [ ] The current hosting-retention notice was reported and a local copy preserved.

## Recovery evidence

- [ ] Existing jobs were checked before retries; successful and approved assets were preserved.
- [ ] Retry limits and the separate pending-job waiting limit were respected.
- [ ] Any blocker is reported with the observed error and remaining work, without inventing a security diagnosis.
